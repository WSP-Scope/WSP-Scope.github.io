The idea

Model adaptation,
through direct weight edits.

Weight-Space Programming (WSP) lets a reasoning agent inspect a model, diagnose its behavior, and directly modify selected weights, without gradients or optimizers.

We follow 16 editing trajectories on Qwen3.5-0.8B and Qwen3.5-9B to examine performance gains, cross-task transfer, and the reliability of finding useful edits.

How it works

How weight-space
editing works.

GPT-6 Astra proposes weight edits and tests them using development feedback. Editing and selection use development data; held-out tests provide a separate evaluation.

Technical & evaluation details
θt+1 = bf16(θt + Δθt)

Updates are computed in float32 and stored in bfloat16. Editing uses no gradients, backward passes, or optimizers.

A constrained editing space

Admissible transformer-block parameters only. Embeddings, final normalization, the LM head, and architecture remain fixed.

Two feedback modes

Generation provides task scores. Teacher forcing returns conditional log-probabilities and activations, not task accuracy.

Held-out comparisons

Test questions are hidden from the editor. Base and edited models are compared under matched evaluation settings.

Beyond the headline result.

Cross-task transfer

One edit can affect other tasks.

A MATH-edited checkpoint can affect scientific QA, instruction following, and scientific reasoning without further editing. Transfer is uneven: a gain on one task does not guarantee improvements everywhere.

See transfer experiments↗
Reliability & limitations

6 of 16 searches keep the original.

Development gains do not always hold up on unseen questions. Finding effective interventions remains inconsistent, and an equal request budget does not mean equal computational cost.
